### Shot design

- **Camera**: three-quarter medium view of a real interior wall. Painter stands next to the actual wall, feet grounded on the floor, full arm/sleeve silhouette visible. Frame composition prioritizes wall contact; the wall surface, roller and painted region occupy the same plane. In portrait, *restage* the shot—do not crop landscape.
- **Clear anatomy**: source-faithful secondary painter design but re-illustrated in clean animation-quality shapes. Forearm, hand and roller are attached through a believable shoulder-elbow-wrist chain. The torso subtly shifts toward the wall; opposite arm balances the motion; small head/gaze shift targets the wet paint.
- **Anticipation** (~0.0–1.0s local): painter looks at the unpainted edge, rotates elbow out, settles the roller against the real wall. No paint appears until the cylinder contacts the wall.
- **Action** (~1.0–3.1s): painter presses and rolls upward, then changes direction. The **wet blue edge tracks the roller's contact line** exactly, showing texture within the rolled area. Roller rotates in the direction of travel. Wrist angle changes consistently. A tiny amount of resistance/squash at contact is visible.
- **Overlap/follow-through** (~3.1–4.4s): elbow and shoulder catch up with the wrist; painter adjusts stance. One subtle paint drip and short wet edge remain attached to the painted surface, not to the tool sprite.
- **Second stroke and exit** (~4.4–6.4s): roller overlaps the first strip to erase the seam. Painter pulls tool off the wall; only then does the roller stop rotating. The completed wall stays painted when the tool leaves.
- **Settle** (~6.4–7.0s): thoughtful look at the real coverage; no looped idle bounce or exaggerated mugging.

### Drawing and rig requirements

Use editable original pose drawings at contact, max reach, bent return, and tool-lift extremes with cleanup and in-betweens; artwork must look cohesive when paused at arbitrary frames. Maintain depth via separate foreground painter, wall and background layers. Hands need a real grip around the roller handle (visible thumb and finger overlap), not a rounded mitten. Add intentional brush/roller texture, restrained line-weight variation, clear lighting, and room perspective. Avoid floating UI cards, radial diagram labels, decorative text, generic SVG stick-limbs, giant square paint patches and random zooms. No substituted Evan art or fabricated customer claims.

### Kill conditions (evaluate on actual frames and motion)

Reject this pilot without making a full-length render if any fail:

1. **Physical placement:** the painter is beside the same wall being painted; tool reaches it in perspective, and contact visually reads even when paused.
2. **Surface causality:** paint appears behind the roller contact path and stays on the wall. No separated bars, sudden wipes, or material appearing before the tool touches.
3. **Connected acting:** arm length remains believable, shoulder/elbow/wrist/hand grip are continuously connected, body carries the motion, and the painter reacts to the work.
4. **Readability muted:** three independent still frames at beginning, contact and end explain the action without captions; normal-speed movement is more than a slide or two moving objects.
5. **Style:** coherent, genuinely pleasing cartoon drawing at native frame size and normal playback; not clip-art or awkward mechanical rigging.
6. **Temporal quality:** no teleporting, limb inversion, awkward seam, frozen 3-second holds, or failed scene transition.
7. **Source integrity:** after art passes, use only original authorized narration with exact source-in/out, independently compose landscape and portrait, and run full decoded media verification.

**These are human visual acceptance checks in addition to automated integrity checks.** A numerical test score cannot mark them passed without looking at the film.
