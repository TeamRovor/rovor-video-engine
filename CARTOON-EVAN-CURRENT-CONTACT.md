# Current-frame F/V contact in the layered jaw experiment

The previous source-matched exposure deliberately rejected full F/V because its jaw used `last_painted_target`, which is not refreshed when the contact renderer bypasses painted vowel construction. This optional review tool captures the actual current oral target before material construction, so contact geometry never depends on a preceding vowel frame. Existing default profiles and the original guarded experiments are unchanged.

`current_contact_exposure.py` reuses the same source-matched jaw, neck, upper-face ownership and painted cavity. The foreground retains the existing continuously blended contact artwork and its registered tooth treatment. At the exact full-contact endpoint, the backing remains the same painted cavity used immediately before that endpoint. The first pilot switched backing material there and produced a 9/255 pixel discontinuity; the corrected endpoint differs by at most 2/255. This is material continuity, not a claim of anatomical accuracy. No retiming, source-alpha modification, opacity repair or new artwork.

## Critical result and next work

The layered experiment can now evaluate F/V without stale geometry or an endpoint backing switch. It still looks too much like a narrow smiling tooth seam. There is little convincing lower-lip tuck, roll or pressure against the incisors. The borrowed upper-mouth artwork and combined jaw/lower-lip drawing do not provide independent anatomical control. The angular jaw shading, chin drift and weak neck cast shadow diverge from the more appealing concept expressions. Static head/eyes in this isolated test do not establish entertaining acting. Positive triangles and opaque coverage do not solve these visible defects.

Do not promote this tool to a release. Next work should establish a clearly designed contact drawing and release drawing with the lower lip wrapping beneath a stable upper-incisor plane, registered to the existing corners/chin. This needs artist redraw and animation review; another numerical blend or extracted seam is insufficient. Compare a controlled contact–release–vowel strip at native scale before extending the complete thought. Existing script/acoustic timings and their 18 visually reviewed but unverified word regions remain unchanged. CPU hardware is adequate for these short proofs; artwork and animation design remain the limiting factors. Main, V3/V4, approval/updater paths and native Moho projects remain untouched.
